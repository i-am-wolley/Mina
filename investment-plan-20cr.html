02 — Market research

Where India and global markets actually stand today

The scenario CAGRs below aren't guesses — each is anchored to one of these findings. Full citations at the bottom of each card.

India — Large-cap

Fairly valued, the best risk-adjusted bet on the table

Nifty 50 PE ≈ 20.4 (Aug 2026), below its 10-year median of 23.4. From this valuation band, historical 5-year forward returns have median'd ~9.6% and were positive in 100% of windows. Paired with India's ~9–10% nominal GDP growth, this is the closest thing to a structurally-supported core holding in the household.

Sources: indexpe.in, trendlyne.com
India — Mid & Smallcap

Already expensive — and you're already overweight here

Nifty Midcap 150 trades near PE 27, Smallcap 250 near PE 38 — both well above their ~17–20x long-run averages. You already hold Tata Midcap150, HDFC Midcap, and Axis Smallcap. Adding fresh money here means doubling down on the household's most stretched valuation, not diversifying it.

Sources: Finomics Edge, Whalesbook
US — S&P 500 / Nasdaq

2nd-highest valuation in 140 years — and a concentration risk you already hold directly

Shiller CAPE ≈ 41.6 (Aug 2026), behind only Dec-1999. Shiller's own model implies ~1.3%/yr over the next decade; JPMorgan and Vanguard cluster at 4–6%. Separately, the top 10 S&P names are ~40% of the index and AI/semis bubble-risk indicators are elevated — and your 13 direct US holdings (NVDA, AMD, MU, TSM, AVGO, ASML, MRVL, ALAB…) plus the AB InBev RSU are already concentrated exactly there.

Sources: GuruFocus, The Motley Fool, Forbes
Emerging Markets ex-India

Cheaper, but the forecast is muted too — a small satellite, not a core bet

EM ex-US trades at a discount to the US on earnings/book value, but 10-year forward return consensus (Vanguard-style capital market assumptions) sits at just 2–4% — valuations have already rallied. The offsetting positive: 2026 consensus EPS growth for EM (~21%) beats the US (~15%) and developed markets (~13%), which is why this earns a modest allocation slot, not a large one.

Sources: State Street, J.P. Morgan
India — Macro tailwind

The structural case underneath the large-cap allocation

Real GDP growth forecast at ~6.8–6.9% for 2026–27 (Goldman Sachs, IMF), nominal GDP growth ~9–10%. Equity returns over long horizons tend to track nominal earnings growth — this is the fundamental tailwind the large-cap allocation is actually betting on, not momentum.

Sources: Goldman Sachs, Trading Economics
Gold

Real bull run under way — but deliberately excluded here

J.P. Morgan targets $6,000/oz by end-2026; long-run historical trend ~8%/yr. Your existing HDFC Gold ETF holding (₹32.4L) keeps compounding at that trend in the model below. Per your instruction, no new money in this plan goes to gold, FDs, or any other safety/diversifier bucket — this is a pure growth allocation.

Sources: J.P. Morgan, GoldRepublic

07 — After the first 6 months

Not a new allocation — a decision framework

There's no fixed reallocation date. Instead, revisit this plan every 6 months against these triggers:

01

Re-run this model with real numbers

Swap in the actual portfolio value and current index PE/CAPE levels every 6 months. The scenario bands here are a snapshot of Sep 2026 valuations, not a permanent forecast.

02

Nifty 50 PE below ~17–18 → tilt more into India large-cap

A genuine cheapening of the core holding is the signal to increase that allocation, not a fixed calendar date.

03

Nifty 50 PE above ~25 → redirect fresh large-cap money to global/EM instead

Don't keep buying the same slice at a richer price just because it's the "core" holding.

04

US CAPE meaningfully mean-reverts (toward 25–30) → that's when to add global exposure

At today's CAPE of 41.6, staying underweight global/AI-concentrated exposure is deliberate, not permanent.

05

12–18 months in, check which scenario you're tracking

If actual returns are running closer to the pessimistic path, decide then — increase the SIP, extend the horizon, or accept a lower number — rather than discovering it in year 9.

06

Trim the RSU concentration over time

AB InBev RSU is a single-stock position outside this whole model. Periodically selling and reinvesting into the diversified allocation above reduces a real, uncompensated risk.

07

Don't add a third overlapping fund

Keerthana's two Parag Parikh Flexi Cap folios already share one ISIN. Consolidate existing overlap before adding anything new in the same category.

08

Re-check the SEBI overseas cap and consolidate if it eases

If a paused India-domiciled global/EM fund reopens, decide whether to keep the direct LRS/ETF holdings (VXUS/VWO/IEMG) or migrate future contributions into the simpler INR wrapper — existing units don't need to move either way.

09

Once the systematic base is established, consider a small direct-stock satellite

Optional, not required — a handful of quality India large-caps in sectors the household doesn't already own heavily (financials, consumer, healthcare — not more semis/tech) can sit alongside the fund-based core. Keep it a satellite (single-digit % of new money), not a replacement for the diversified allocation above.

08 — Assumptions & methodology

Every rate used, and why

Compounding
All figures use monthly compounding (annual rate ÷ 12) for consistency between the lump-sum buckets and the growing SIP annuity — not mixed annual/monthly conventions.

Value-weighted average of the three real, named FDs on file (Shriram Finance 7.6%, HDFC Bank 7.75% and 7.25%) — contracted rates, held constant regardless of scenario.
EPF — existing balance ₹53.1L @ 8.25%/yr
Current EPFO statutory declared rate, applied to the balance already on file.
EPF — new contributions ₹69,400/month @ 8.25%/yr
₹34,700 employee + ₹34,700 employer, modeled as a flat monthly contribution (no growth) compounding at the same statutory rate. In reality this rises with salary increments, so this is a conservative floor, not an overstatement.
Annual lumpsum — ₹10,00,000 every May
Modeled as a single lumpsum injection each May (first: May 2027), invested in the same allocation as the monthly SIP and compounding at the same scenario rate — flat ₹10L/year, no step-up assumed.
Gold — 8%/yr
Long-run historical trend rate, not the much higher 2026 spot forecasts (JPMorgan's $6,000/oz end-2026 target implies a much faster near-term move) — a 10-year CAGR assumption shouldn't extrapolate one bull year.
